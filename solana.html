01The desk

One trade, sixteen specialists.

AUSTIN POWERZ reads pump.fun’s own launch feed and its minute candles — roughly 380 coins a sweep, some of them two minutes old — kills most of what it finds with code that costs nothing, and hands the survivors to five analysts who are not allowed to read each other. Lease a floor and yours copies that desk — sized by you, traded from your own wallet, and sold on its band’s clockite, your categories, your launchpads. You trade SOL from your own wallet; the desk holds no keys and signs nothing.

Every cycle begins wide, and on pump.fun only. The desk reads pump.fun’s own launch feed and the minute candles behind it, alongside twenty-five search angles, and comes back with roughly 380 coins worth a first look — including ones created in the last few minutes, which a search engine cannot see yet.

Then the cheapest seat does most of the killing. MAXWELL SMORT is deterministic code — no model, no mood, no bill — enforcing hard floors: liquidity, age, wash ratio, and a real buy-then-sell probe that proves an exit actually exists. About 79% of candidates die here, at a cost of nothing.

The survivors go to five analysts — forensics, liquidity, flow, narrative, technical — who work blind to one another and are expected to disagree. That is the design: agreement is only worth something when it wasn't arranged. A full workup costs $1.29 to $1.44 in model time; a whole cycle lands around $4.

The suspicion earns its keep. The desk's best catch was a token showing $1.8 million of daily volume that turned out to be 57,479 trades averaging $31.36 against a $64k reserve — turnover wearing depth's coat. No single seat is credited with the catch; the pipeline is the instrument.

The desk also distrusts its own instruments. One pool once quoted RAY at $4,064.74 while the real price was $0.81; every price the desk uses is now a median taken across the deepest pools. When a number is surprising, the ruler is the first suspect.

Whatever remains walks the gauntlet: AGENT 48 attacks it, JAMES BOUND may not propose until he states how the attack was answered, and JUDGE DREAD — code, not a model — can veto it before BIG C ever sees it. Only BIG C turns research into a call, and what leaves the building is an unsigned ticket. Always.
